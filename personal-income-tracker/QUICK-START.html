Income Tracker

Quick Start · Standalone · Runs offline

1First-time setup — do this once

  1. Save index.html to a permanent folder (e.g. "Income Tracker"). Don't move or rename it afterward — the browser ties your saved data to that exact file.
  2. Double-click it to open in your browser. No internet needed.
  3. Click Restore (top-right) → choose your data file income-tracker-…-migrated.json. Loads all years.
  4. It now saves automatically as you type. Reopen the same file next time.

Income tab — your dashboard

  • Pick the Year (top-left). 2026 shows the live projection; past years show actual totals.
  • Weekly entry grid: type hours per site — dollars auto-fill from the rate. Clear both cells to remove a week. Add week / +7 days add rows.
  • Sites & rates: change a rate, add a mid-year rate (each rate has its own weeks + average), or change how a site projects (run-rate / actual hours / manual).
  • Year-over-year and Total projection by year tables — click any cell to edit.

Kettering detail tab

Timeclock & pay periods

  • New pay period → Add entry → type date, clock in/out, pick GK or RO.
  • Hours and Week 1/2 fill in automatically; tiles total GK/RO per week + grand total.
  • Click Push GK/RO hrs → Income grid to send those hours to the Income tab (no double-typing).

Charts by day & Co-Reg

  • Add a week, enter RO charts by day + Co-Reg + hours. Season totals & per-day averages compute at the bottom.

DP PRN Log tab

  • Toggle OnSite ↔ Remote at the top — each is saved separately.
  • New week (any date snaps to Monday). Page through weeks with the dropdown.
  • Fill the per-day task grid (Closeout, Fusions, SPC, Peer, IMRT QA, Plan Checks, SRS/SBRT, Hours).
  • Use the free-text box under each day for anything not in the table.
  • Fill Totals by location (hours). Export CSV for a copy.

!The one rule: Backup

Your data lives only in that browser. Click Backup regularly — it saves a dated .json copy you control.

  • It's your safety copy if the browser is ever cleared (then just Restore it).
  • It's how you move to a new computer: copy both files over, open, Restore.
  • Restore replaces everything — back up before restoring an older file.
